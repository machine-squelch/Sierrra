Website & Google — Owner checklist

Prepared for Sierra Heavy Duty RV & Truck Center (Sonora, CA). Work through the items below so your site, Google Business Profile, and customer experience stay aligned. Check boxes as you complete each step.

1. Google Business Profile (GBP)

  • Claim or verify the profile; confirm you are an authorized manager.
  • Business name matches your legal / on-signage name (no extra keywords stuffed into the title).
  • Address & pin match where customers arrive; fix the map if the pin is wrong.
  • Primary phone is the main shop line customers call; additional numbers added as secondary if needed.
  • Website URL points to your real domain (production). Update when moving from a preview domain to the final one.
  • Hours (including holiday / seasonal updates) match your website and what staff tell callers.
  • Add services and photos (shop exterior, bay, team, finished work) — strong trust signals.

2. Business categories — is “AutoRepair” right?

On the website (schema.org): We use AutoRepair plus LocalBusiness. That is a valid type for a licensed repair shop that works on motor vehicles, including RVs and trucks. It helps search engines understand “repair business at this address.”

On Google Business Profile: Categories come from Google’s fixed list, not schema.org. Pick the one primary category that best describes your main business (for example, RV repair shop, Truck repair shop, or Auto repair shop, depending on what you want to rank for most). Add secondary categories for other services. If unsure, choose the category that matches how most customers describe you when they call.

  • Review primary + secondary GBP categories with your team.
  • Confirm categories match the services described on your website.

3. Make it easy for visitors to leave a Google review

Google’s review link is unique to your business. In Google Business Profile: Ask for reviews → copy your short link. Paste it in the blanks below and use it everywhere.

Tactic What to do
Direct link Put your review URL on the website (e.g. “Review us on Google” in the footer or after service). Owner: paste link here: _________________________
QR code Print a QR that opens the review link — counter card, lobby, invoice, or completion paperwork.
Thank-you moment When the job is done and the customer is happy, ask in person: “We’d appreciate a quick Google review — I can text you the link.”
Follow-up text / email Same-day or next-day message with the link (use your normal CRM or personal process; avoid bulk spam).
Never Do not offer money or gifts for reviews (against Google policy). Do not write fake reviews or review your own business.
  • Create and save the official Google review link.
  • Add a visible “Leave a review” button or link on the website (footer or contact page).
  • Print QR code for the front counter or customer copies.
  • Brief staff on when and how to ask (after a positive handoff).

4. Keep information consistent (NAP + ratings)

  • Name, address, phone (NAP) are identical on the website, GBP, Facebook, and printed materials.
  • If you display an average star rating or review count on the site, it should match (or be clearly sourced from) what Google shows, or update the site to match.
  • When hours or phone numbers change, update Google first, then the website (or ask AdamsLocal to update the site config).

5. Domain & launch

  • Confirm which domain is “live” for customers vs. a temporary preview domain.
  • After switching to the final domain, set the correct URL in GBP and in hosting environment variables.
  • Test contact links: tap-to-call, email, and map directions from a phone.

6. Optional next steps with your web team

  • Replace placeholder contact/booking forms with real email or shop management integration.
  • Add your Google Business Profile “share” URL into the site config for richer search connections.
  • Embed Google’s map widget using the real place embed code (pin accuracy).
  • Connect Google Search Console on the production domain to monitor visibility.
